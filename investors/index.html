The story

A month ago we had one website

Three of us: two IIM Indore classmates and a flatmate found through a Reddit post. We all have day jobs. Asanoha gets the nights and the weekends.

In September we built the store site. In October we wrote seven commits. Then in November we stopped talking and started shipping: Wearday on the 6th, People Pitchers on the 10th, Kosmik on the 13th, the LLP on the 14th and our own payment rail rewritten from scratch on the 22nd.

We keep receipts. Every line on the right links back to a commit, a date or a registration you can check.

Next, one launch at a time: Kosmik goes public on 1 December, People Pitchers opens to everyone on 14 December, and the store opens orders only after GST registration, licence details on every product and legal sign-off.

The founders

A Reddit post, a classroom and a living room

None of us met at a startup event. We met the way people in India actually meet: looking for a flatmate and sitting in the same class.

01 · S

Nine people answered a flatmate post

A needed a flatmate in Bengaluru and posted on Reddit. Nine people replied. S got the room.

The flatmate turned out to be the person who asks what everything costs, who will do the paperwork and whether anyone has eaten. Every team needs that person. Ours came from a Reddit thread, and today he runs operations.

02 · J

A classmate who asks how everyone feels

At IIM Indore, A found J: an engineer turned HR consultant, the kind of person who asks how everyone feels, even in a meeting about fonts.

Our first product together wasn’t for investors. It was for our own batch. The timetable lived in a spreadsheet that kept changing, so we built IIMXamday: every classmate gets their own calendar, readings, course groups and a jobs board, updated every morning at 6.

03 · The plan

Late night, chai, “yaar, hear me out”

The plan came together in a living room, laptops open. A had the idea. J asked who it was for. S asked what it would cost.

We split the work the only way that works with day jobs. Everyone owns one thing, and nobody waits for permission.

04 · Then we built

One month, four products, one company

Weeknights went into code and weekends into shipping. The odd weekend went up a trail instead, and we came back and shipped anyway.

In November that became 331 commits, four products, our own payment rail and Asanoha LLP. We are not asking you to believe a plan. We are showing you what three people did with one month and no money.

Layer 2

The money-makers: what AWS is to Amazon

People Pitchers

Live, invite-only · public on 14 Dec

One hiring platform for India. Recruiters hire from scored work, not keywords. Job seekers get a jobs feed, a resume studio, a browser extension and Learn. Next: one assessment engine for Learn, Proof projects and company tests.

Real7,245jobs indexed from 2,873 companies
Real69Learn lessons in 7 tracks, plus 8 timed assessments
Plan~₹1running cost per candidate
Plan~10 minfrom a job description to a draft test, a human approves every item

The plan, in four moves

  1. One engine. Learn exams, Proof projects and company tests run on the same assessment engine, with 16 question types as plug-ins.
  2. AI builds, humans decide. The builder asks 3 to 6 questions, drafts the test and checks itself. Nothing goes live without a person approving it.
  3. Proctoring lite. Tab switches, paste logs and face count in the browser. No face recognition, no emotion scoring. A trust report, never a verdict.
  4. Colleges free, companies pay. Colleges are the channel, job seekers never pay to be tested, companies pay to hire.

How it scales

  1. Start where we are known. Our IIM Indore batch already uses what we build. Placement cells, then the companies that hire from them, come next for People Pitchers.
  2. One account, many products. Someone who joins for one product is one tap from the others, so each new user costs us once, not five times.
  3. Our own rails, low running cost. Payments, sign-in, WhatsApp and AI caching are ours and shared, so revenue grows faster than our bills.
  4. Then sell the rails. Noha Pay turns what we built for ourselves into a product for other small merchants.

How we build

Every product makes the next one cheaper

The revolution is boring on purpose. We build a piece once, then every product reuses it. That is why the fourth product took days, not months.

One account

Sign in once with Google or an emailed code, and it works on the store, Kosmik and Wearday.

One rail and one wallet

Noha Pay takes UPI for every product, and Noha Money carries cashback between them.

Library before model

Kosmik answers from its library and cache first. People Pitchers caches every paid AI and data call for 30 days.

Free tiers, on purpose

Cloudflare Workers, Supabase and a free Google Cloud machine. We pay for usage, not for idle servers.

Questions

What investors ask us

Short answers here. The long ones are better on a call.

Why four products and not one?

Because the expensive parts are shared. Sign-in, payments, the wallet, WhatsApp messages and AI caching were built once. A new product starts with all of them, so we can test a market in days and keep only what works. Each product still has its own name, its own users and its own way to make money.

Isn’t that unfocused?

The focus is the platform under the products, not one app. Our measure is simple: how fast a new idea reaches paying users, and how little it costs us to run. Products that don’t earn their place will be shut down, not kept alive.

How do you make money?

Subscriptions (People Pitchers seats, Kosmik passes, Wearday Premium), one-off purchases (Kosmik reports, store orders), hiring fees and escrow commission on People Pitchers, and later a hosted Noha Pay for other merchants.

What traction is real today?

The numbers above are read from our databases, not estimated: 7,245 jobs from 2,873 companies, 179 job seeker profiles, 2,699 cited library entries, 27 WhatsApp-verified people in the Kosmik alpha, and IIMXamday live for our batch of 190. Revenue is still at the test stage. We would rather show you small true numbers than big round ones.

How big can this get?

Our year-three targets add up to about ₹30 crore a year, and each needs less than 1% of its market. The markets are proven: Naukri earned ₹2,343 crore from recruiters in FY26 and Astrotalk ₹1,176 crore in FY25. The math for every number is in the market section above.

How will you get users without a big ad budget?

Through people who already know us: our IIM Indore batch, their placement cells and the companies that hire from them. Kosmik answers in Hindi and Hinglish, where most apps don’t. And one Asanoha account means a user who joins one product can try the others for free.

Why now?

UPI made small payments free to take, AI made one engineer worth a team, and Indian users now expect apps in their own language. A small team can now ship what needed fifty people five years ago.

Why you?

We ship. 364 commits, four products and a registered LLP, with day jobs. One founder writes the code, one handles people and partners, and one runs operations.

What are the risks?

The store sells a regulated Ayurvedic medicine. It is made under an AYUSH manufacturing licence and sold only on a prescription from a registered AYUSH practitioner, and labels and advertising rules come first, and we follow legal advice on each. WhatsApp codes run through our own relay today and should move to the official WhatsApp Business API before we scale. And four products is a lot for three people, which is exactly why we share everything we can.

What is the launch plan?

One public launch at a time. Kosmik goes public on 1 December 2026, People Pitchers opens to everyone on 14 December, and the store opens orders only when GST registration, licence details on every product and legal sign-off are in place. A date can move. The checks before each launch cannot.

What happens after I get in touch?

You confirm your WhatsApp number with a code, so we know the message is really from you. We get your note straight away, and one of the founders writes back to you.
